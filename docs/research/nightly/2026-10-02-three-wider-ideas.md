# Three wider ideas (second round, 2 October 2026)

Kieran's feedback on the first proposal (`2026-10-02-kids-jokes-age-11.md`): too niche (one age), too narrow a net, and no real buying power like the Dave series. Agreed. The Dave books work because the buyer is a grown-up with money, buying a £9.99 gift for a specific person in a very large group. So this round only looks at books that keep that shape, with the biggest groups I can find, all in the Dave voice and look, all text-only inside, all makeable in Book Factory today.

## Decision needed

**Pick one of three: A (retirement), B (football), C (new dad). Reply with the letter, or `none`.** My recommendation is **A**.

## Why these three

Each is a person in a very big group whom someone else buys for:

| | Who is "him" | Who buys | Gift moment | Size of group |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Retirement** | the man who has just stopped work | wife, kids, colleagues' whip-round (leaving gift) | leaving do, birthday, Christmas, Father's Day: all year | most men retire at 65-67; retirees give about £1,300 a year to family (Quilter 2025) |
| **B. Football** | the man for whom football is the week | partner, kids, mates | Christmas, Father's Day, birthdays | 20% of UK adults are "very interested" in football, 83% of them men (Marketing Week) |
| **C. New dad** | the man who is about to be, or just became, a dad | his partner, his mum, friends | baby shower, birth, Father's Day, Christmas | 594,677 births in England and Wales in 2024 (ONS) |

How sure am I: **medium.** Amazon's pages came back patchy tonight, so the competitor numbers are from the 30 September snapshot (`docs/research/2026-09-30-amazon-bestsellers.csv`), which I searched by keyword. Group sizes are from the web sources at the end. I have not checked sales volumes, only list ranks, prices and review counts.

## A. Retirement (recommended)

- **Working title:** *The Newly Retired Man's Guide to Being Home All Day: An Official Handbook for Him, and for the Person Who Now Lives With Him*
- **Pitch:** the Dave formula applied to the one life event that comes with a leaving gift. The retiree starts "helping" in the kitchen, rearranges the cupboards, takes on a "project", and has opinions on a school run that ended years ago. The wife reads it aloud; the colleagues buy it for the card.
- **Why it has buying power:** the retirement books in the 30 September data sell at **$13-15**, well above the Dave price. *Retirement Activity Book for the Newly Retired* (Eight15 Ltd, 168 pages, 118 reviews) is at UK Quiz #17 and Brain Teasers #28. A *Retirement Gift For Woman* activity book (independent, 124 pages, **407 reviews**, $13.50) is at US Crosswords #13. *The Ultimate Fun Retirement Guide for Men* ($14.99, 28 reviews) is in US Sports Humour at #23. *Retirement for Beginners* (hardcover, 785 reviews) is at UK Jokes #19. Small publishers are in there with few reviews, and none of these is a deadpan official manual.
- **Plan:** same format as Golf and Padel (6 x 9 black and white, about 80 pages, £9.99 / $9.99, earning about £4.06 / $3.69). Sections: Day 1 assessment ("Are you ready to be home?"), the Kitchen Boundary Agreement, the Project Register (every "quick job" and how long it really took), a diagnostic of his new routines, a week-by-week Adjustment Plan, and a signed Retirement Treaty. One cover picture (about 2 credits), no interior pictures.
- **Sample lines (original):** "Week 1: The retiree believes he has been given the gift of time. The household believes it has been given a colleague." / "The Dishwasher Loading Review: there are now two methods. Only one is correct, and it has never been yours." / Back cover: "He spent 40 years being needed by a company. He would now like to be needed by a dishwasher."
- **Series fit:** same buyer as Dave (the wife), same look, sits next to Golf and Padel. Evergreen: leaving gifts happen every month. An easy sequel is a "for her" twin.
- **Risk:** the retiree may feel patronised, so keep it affectionate (the household is the joke too).

## B. Football

- **Working title:** *The Football Fan's Guide to Having a Weekend: An Official Programme for the Man Whose Calendar Is the Fixture List*
- **Pitch:** the widest hobby net there is. No club names or badges (that avoids licensing); the jokes work for every fan: the 3pm Saturday blackout, "I'll just check the score", the pub forecast of a 0-0 draw.
- **Evidence:** in UK Humour: Sports, the football titles are kids' books (Football School, Marcus Rashford, Ultimate Football Heroes) and memoirs from big publishers. I found **no adult deadpan football gift book from a small publisher in the top 30**. That could be a gap or a weak market. The 20% "very interested" figure says the audience exists, but the gap is not proven. This is the weakest evidence of the three.
- **Plan:** same format and price. Sections: the Fixture List Treaty, a Matchday Assessment, "Things you said at 4:45pm on a Saturday", a Season Planner that checks family events against fixtures, a Transfer Window Mood Chart, a Referee Complaint Log.
- **Sample lines (original):** "Do not schedule anything between 12:30 and 5pm. He is not available. He is, however, present. Do not confuse the two." / "Wedding in March? Check the fixture list. He already has."
- **Timing:** the season runs August to May, so a November book gets Christmas and the second half of the season.
- **Risk:** football is the most crowded hobby for gift ideas in general, so how well the book gets found on Amazon is the unknown.

## C. New dad

- **Working title:** *The New Dad's Guide to Sleeping Again: An Official Operations Manual for the Man Who Has Just Been Given a Very Small Boss*
- **Pitch:** a baby gift that is for him, not the baby. Dad is the person nobody buys for. Buyers: the partner, grandparents, friends. Evergreen.
- **Evidence:** the US parenting-humour lists show demand: *Dude, You're Gonna Be a Dad!* (3,239 reviews), *Dad Jokes for New Dads* (Sourcebooks, 1,573 reviews), *There Are Dads Way Worse Than You* (1,394 reviews, $7.65). I found **no UK-voiced deadpan version**; the top titles are American in tone.
- **Plan:** same format. Sections: the Handover Assessment ("Are you rested?" No), the Night Shift Rota, a nappy-changing performance review, "Things you said before the baby arrived" (a tick-list), a Sleep Gauge, a cut-out Certificate of Parental Competence. Cover: dad, baby and coffee.
- **Sample lines (original):** "The baby has no schedule. You are expected to adopt it." / "Task: assemble the cot. Estimated time: 20 minutes. Actual time: the length of the pregnancy."
- **Risk:** the baby-gift market is crowded, so a new title needs a strong cover. The US lists show lower prices ($7-8) than retirement.

## Comparison and my pick

| | A. Retirement | B. Football | C. New dad |
|---|---|---|---|
| Size of group | big, steady | biggest | big, steady |
| Price the lists support | $13-15 | unclear | $7-8 (US) |
| Gap in the top lists | yes (no deadpan manual) | looks like yes, unproven | yes in UK voice |
| Buying moments | all year | Christmas, season | all year |
| Fit with Dave | strongest (the wife buys) | good | good |
| Effort | one cover picture | one cover picture | one cover picture |

**Recommendation: A, retirement.** It is the only one where small publishers already sell similar gift books at $13-15, so a £9.99 Dave book is the funny, cheaper option, and the leaving-gift and birthday buyers are there all year. B is the best "second Dave book" if you want the biggest audience. C is the safest evergreen.
