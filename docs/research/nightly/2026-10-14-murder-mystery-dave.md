# Proposal: a Dave-style murder-mystery puzzle book (angle 3, a new line)

Nightly research run, 14 October 2026. Angle 3 of the rotation (the last run was angle 2, the would-you-rather book).

## 1. Decision needed

**Book: *Who Nicked Dave's Putter? A Murder-Mystery Logic Puzzle Book for Households That Have Lost Something to a Hobby*, about 40 short comic "cases" you solve by deduction. It needs two build phases first. Reply `go` or `no` (and why).**

## 2. The pitch

- **Title (working):** *Who Nicked Dave's Putter? 40 Cosy Crimes in the Hobby Addict's Household: A Logic Puzzle Book*
- **Who buys it:** puzzle and cosy-crime fans (the biggest buying group on the lists below), plus the usual Dave buyers (wives, partners, grown-up kids) as a gift for the hobby-obsessed man who "likes a puzzle".
- **When:** year-round (puzzle books sell all year, per the 30 September report), with a Christmas push. Realistically live **mid-to-late November**, so it catches the end of Christmas, then Valentine's and Father's Day.
- **Why now:** murder-mystery puzzles are the hottest corner of the puzzle lists. Of the 10 top-10 places I counted in US Logic and US Puzzles, most are Murdoku, Murdle-style or "Killer" books.
- **What is different:** every one of them is a straight whodunit. None is a comedy about a household where the "crime" is a missing putter, a lost parking space or who ate the last of the cheese. Ours is funny first, solvable second, and keeps the Dave look.

What this says:

- **Small publishers can reach the top.** Kill Grid is **#1 US Word Search with only 49 reviews**, a few weeks after launch. "The Killer Never Checked In" is in three top-20 lists with 64 reviews at $6.99.
- **The big names are far ahead on reviews** (Murdle 3,163; Murdoku 1,024), so we would not beat them head-on. Our route is the niche: comedy plus the Dave household.
- **Prices are $10-15**, well above a normal puzzle book, so £8.99-9.99 is normal.
- **Crowding risk:** about 20 murder-mystery titles on the lists, many under 3 months old. The trend may already be peaking. A funny angle is a reason to stand out, not a guarantee.
- **Murdle sells to a gift crowd** and a Murdle Junior exists, so the "household" version is plausible, but I have no proof a *comedy* version sells. That is the real unknown.

## 4. The book plan

- **Format:** 6 x 9 in, black and white, about **104 pages** (kept at or under 110 so printing stays at the flat low rate; the first report, `docs/research/2026-09-30-new-book-types.md`, has the royalty table, and web sources disagree on KDP's current fixed charge, so check KDP's calculator before pricing).
- **Price and earnings:** UK £8.99 / US $9.99. Roughly £3 and $3.69 a copy at the first report's rates.
- **Pen name:** the Dave brand (Kieran Smith), same big-lettering cover. The kids' brand question does not arise.
- **Contents:**
  1. How to play, and the cast (Dave; his wife; teenage son; mother-in-law; the neighbour; the dog).
  2. Four "Episodes" of 10 cases each, one running story per episode (the missing putter; the 6am alarm; the garage; the holiday that was "near a course"). Cases rise from Easy to Hard.
  3. Each case is one page: a short story, a clue list, and a deduction grid (who, what, where) to tick off, with a "Name the culprit" box.
  4. A final "Grand Reveal" case that ties the four episodes together.
  5. Full solutions at the back (each with a one-line joke verdict).
- **Pictures:** none inside. One cover picture (about 2 credits). I could not read the Higgsfield balance (connector not available in this run); last known 6 credits, and the Retirement cover may use some.
- **Cover direction:** a "crime scene" kitchen: chalk outline of a putter on the hall carpet, police tape across the hat stand, Dave looking innocent, an empty band at the top for the big title.

## 5. Samples (original)

**Back cover:** "The putter has gone. The shed is locked. Everyone in the house had a reason, and none of them is 'he left it at the course', which is what he will say in an hour. Forty cases. One household. Solve them before Dave tells you how he'd have done it."

**Sample case 1 (Easy): The Missing Putter**
Suspects: Dave, Sue (wife), Callum (son), Gran. Places: shed, boot, hall, kitchen. Clues:
1. The putter was not in the shed, and the person who hid it is not Sue.
2. Gran was in the kitchen all morning and has never touched a golf club "on principle".
3. Callum was in the hall; he did not hide the putter, but he did see who went to the boot.
4. Whoever was in the boot is the person who last "needed a quick look" at it.
*Who has it, and where?* (Answer: Dave, in the boot. Verdict: "Case closed. He reported it stolen to feel included.")

**Sample case 2 (Medium): The 6am Alarm.** Four suspects, four rooms, one alarm clock set to a time nobody admits to. Clue: "The person in the bathroom has never, in 22 years, set an alarm for a time starting with a 5."

**Sample verdict lines:** "Culprit: the neighbour. Motive: wanted a lift to the club." / "Culprit: Dave. Again. Sentence: one weekend without mentioning it."

I wrote these to show tone only. A real book needs every case checked by a program to confirm there is exactly one answer (see section 6).

## 6. What Book Factory needs

Book Factory can typeset everything *except* the puzzles themselves. Build work first:

- **Phase A: a logic-puzzle generator.** A script that builds a deduction case (suspects, places, items), writes clues from a joke template list, and **proves the solution is unique**. A puzzle with two answers is the number-one complaint in puzzle-book reviews. Includes a test that solves every case independently.
- **Phase B: the puzzle page.** A new `activity` block type for the deduction grid and the "name the culprit" box, set as real type, plus the solutions pages. (The grid can start as a table; `docs/RENDERING.md` lists what exists.) The picture budget is unaffected (no pictures).
- Then the ordinary run: `/write-book` for the story text and verdicts (about 3 sittings), cover with one picture, QA and upload.

This is the first big build since the CRM mockup, and **the Phase 21 CRM is still open in `PLAN.md`**, so if Kieran says `go`, he chooses whether to pause it (`CLAUDE.md`: protect the open phase). The puzzle page would also serve the Golf Addict's Puzzle Book idea already parked in `IDEAS.md`.
