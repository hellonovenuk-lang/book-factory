## 2. The pitch

- **Title (working):** *The Hobby Addict's Annual Performance Review: A Formal Appraisal, to Be Completed by the Household and Handed Over in Silence*
- **Who buys it:** his wife, partner or grown-up kids, for any man with a hobby (golf, running, padel, cycling, fishing, the shed). They fill it in with a pen before giving it, so the gift is personal and takes ten minutes.
- **When:** no single deadline. It suits a birthday, an anniversary, Valentine's (14 February 2027) and Father's Day (20 June 2027). Christmas 2026 is already missed.
- **Why now:** fill-in books for couples are selling on Amazon tonight from small publishers with few reviews, and every fill-in book for dad is sentimental ("what I love about you"). Nobody has done the deadpan office appraisal.
- **Different from Amazon:** the others ask you to say something nice. This one is a mock HR form (HR is the office department that runs staff reviews): ratings, targets, a disciplinary note and a certificate. It works for every hobby, so one book covers all Dave buyers.

## 4. The book plan

- **Format:** 6 x 9 in, black and white, about 72 pages. £8.99 UK / $9.99 US. By the royalty rule in `docs/research/2026-09-30-new-book-types.md` (60% of the price, less £1.93 printing) that is about £3.46 a copy in the UK and $3.69 in the US. This is my sum from that rule, not a figure in the table.
- **Pen name:** the Dave brand. No named main character is needed: the form has a blank for "Name of employee".
- **Shape:** an official appraisal pack. Every page has printed jokes to read (guidance notes, rating scales, small print), plus boxes to tick and a few lines to write on. Roughly two thirds reading, one third filling in.
- **Contents:**
  1. Front matter: how to complete this review; who may act as the panel; confidentiality (there is none)
  2. **Part 1 Employee details.** Name, hobby, years of service, job title he thinks he has, job title the household uses.
  3. **Part 2 Attendance.** Days present in body; days present in mind; a table of approved and unapproved absences ("just nine holes").
  4. **Part 3 Core duties.** Twelve duties rated 1 to 5 with printed guidance for each score: bins, listening, the thing he said he would fix, being told about plans, remembering them.
  5. **Part 4 Communication.** A gauge of how long he can talk before the hobby comes up; a tick list of phrases used this year.
  6. **Part 5 Expenses.** A claims table: item, what he said it cost, what it cost. Auditor's note.
  7. **Part 6 Conduct.** One formal warning (cut-out card), one commendation, mitigating circumstances.
  8. **Part 7 Objectives for next year.** Three targets set by the panel, in the approved office style, with space for his.
  9. **Part 8 Employee's right of reply.** Four lines. Guidance: "Use them wisely."
  10. Back matter: overall rating, a certificate of continued employment, signatures of the panel, and an appeal form that is already stamped "Declined".
- **Pictures:** none inside, or six small part-opener pictures (about 12 credits) if Kieran wants the Dave look. I recommend none inside: a form is funnier plain. Cover picture: 2 credits, plus redraws.
- **Cover direction:** made to look like an office document that has got out of hand: big title lettering, a "CONFIDENTIAL" stamp set as real type, and one full-colour family scene in the Dave style (the family sat in a row at the kitchen table like an interview panel, him opposite in golf shoes). Gift line: "The gift for the man who has everything except feedback."

## 5. Samples (original, first drafts)

**Back cover:** "Every year he reviews his own performance, out loud, in the car. This year the household would like a turn. This is a formal appraisal, to be completed by the people who live with him and handed over without comment. It covers attendance, core duties, communication, expenses and conduct. A pen is required. He has a right of reply. It is four lines long."

**How to complete this review (front matter).** This form is completed by the panel. The panel is whoever lives in the house and can hold a pen. Complete it in private, at the kitchen table, while the employee is out. He is out. Answer honestly. Where two members of the panel disagree, the one who does the washing has the casting vote. Hand the finished review to the employee on a day of your choosing. Do not explain it. He will read Part 5 first.

**Part 2, Attendance.** Tick one. Over the past year the employee was present at home: [ ] most weekends. [ ] some weekends. [ ] for the parts of weekends that fell between other things. [ ] He was in the building. *Guidance:* being in the garage counts as present. Being in the garage "sorting the bag" counts as present, with a note on file.

**Part 3, Core duty 4: Listening.** Circle a score. **1** He did not hear the question. **2** He heard the question and answered a different one. **3** He answered the question. **4** He answered the question and remembered the answer on Thursday. **5** Reserved for exceptional cases. The panel has never awarded a 5 and does not expect to.

**Part 5, Expenses.** Enter three items bought this year. Item: ______. What he said it cost: £____. What it cost: £____. *Auditor's note:* the phrase "it was in the sale" is not a receipt. Neither is "I've had that ages".

**Formal warning (cut-out card).** "This is your ______ warning. It concerns ______. The panel notes that you said it would not happen again in ______ (year)." Signed: ______.

(These show the idea and voice only; the manuscript pass checks them against the Dave voice bible, the banned phrases and content QA.)

## 6. What Book Factory needs

- **Today:** almost everything. The `activity` page type already has the blocks this book is made of (`docs/RENDERING.md`): tick statements, checklists, a score line, write-in lines, tables with empty write-in cells, a gauge, a cut-out card and signature lines; the certificate page type exists too. `/write-book` can start it.
- **Two things to check on the first sample page, not build phases:** (a) there is no "circle a number 1 to 5" block, so ratings would be set as a small table with tick boxes; (b) the page-plan command expects `## Stage N` chapters, and this book has "Parts", which is only a naming choice in the manuscript.
- **A KDP rule to respect.** Amazon calls a book "low-content" when the pages are mostly empty for the buyer to fill, and it lists "prompt journals" and "coupon books" by name. Low-content books lose the free ISBN (the book's ID number), cannot be in a series, and get no "look inside" sample. Puzzle and activity books are not low-content. So this book must be a humour book you also write in: real printed copy on every page, never rows of blank lines. That is the plan above, and it is also the funnier book. The final call is Amazon's.
- **A knock-on for an older proposal:** the 30 October voucher book would count as a "coupon book" under that same rule. Worth knowing before saying go to that one.
