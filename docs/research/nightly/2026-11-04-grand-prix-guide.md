# Proposal: a Dave-style guide for the Grand Prix fan in the house (angle 1, Dave series)

Nightly research run, 4 November 2026. Angle 1 of the rotation (the last run was angle 3, the dog owner's guide).

## 1. Decision needed

**Book: *The Grand Prix Addict's Guide to Sunday Lunch*, a straight-faced official programme for the man whose roast is timed around the start lights. Reply `go` or `no` (and why).**

## 2. The pitch

- **Title (working):** *The Grand Prix Addict's Guide to Sunday Lunch: An Official Programme for the Man Who Knows Every Team Principal and None of the Neighbours*
- **Who buys it:** his partner, his mum or a friend, for the F1 fan in the house. Motor racing is now a mainstream UK habit: Sky's 2025 season was its most-watched ever (162 million viewer hours across UK and Ireland), and Sky now holds the rights to 2034.
- **When:** the 2027 season starts in March, so it suits birthdays, Mother's Day (7 March 2027, partners buying for him) and Father's Day (Sunday 20 June 2027, my own count). It cannot make Christmas 2026 (section 7).
- **Why now:** the sport's UK audience has grown fast and younger; the rights deal means it is not going away.
- **Different from Amazon:** the gift books I found for F1 fans are history, memoir and stats (The Little Book of Formula One, a Guenther Steiner memoir, children's guides). I found no deadpan manual aimed at the household of the fan.
- **Name care:** "Formula 1" and "F1" are trademarks. The title uses "Grand Prix" and no logos, drivers' names or team names, the same care as the Hyrox proposal.

## 4. The book plan

- **Format:** 6 x 9 in, black and white, about 80 pages. £9.99 UK / $9.99 US, earning about £4.06 / $3.69 a copy (royalty table in `docs/research/2026-09-30-new-book-types.md`; check KDP's calculator at upload).
- **Pen name:** the Dave brand, same big-lettering cover. Dave gets a new hobby; the family stays the same.
- **Contents (numbered activities, as in the live books):**
  1. Welcome to the Programme (the race is at 2pm; so is lunch)
  2. Stage 1: Race Day Scheduling (lunch moved to 4:15, "after the podium")
  3. Stage 2: Terminology ("box box", "undercut", "it's lights out and away we go" said to the dog)
  4. Stage 3: Strategy (the pit-stop plan for the school run)
  5. Stage 4: Spending (the team shirt, the cap, the cap for the cap)
  6. Stage 5: Holidays (a week in Belgium "for the track")
  7. Stage 6: Commentary (explaining tyre compounds to people who asked about the weather)
  8. Stage 7: Qualifying (Saturday also belongs to him)
  9. Diagnostic: "Are you a Fan or a Race Engineer?"; cut-out "Certificate of Parc Fermé" (the sofa spot nobody may touch)
- **Picture budget:** none inside (typeset activity pages). One cover picture, about 2 credits. I could not read the Higgsfield balance (connector not available); last known 6 credits in `PLAN.md`, so a cover picture is affordable.
- **Cover direction:** big title, one family scene: Dave on the sofa in a team shirt facing the TV, the roast going cold on the table, his wife standing with the gravy boat. A plain empty band across the top for the title.

## 5. Samples (original)

**Back cover:** "Dave has watched 23 races this year and attended zero parents' evenings. The roast is in at 11, out at 2, and back in at 4:15 'after the podium'. He once explained a tyre strategy to a man who had asked whether the car park was free. This is the official programme for getting him from the grid to the gravy."

**Three sample activities:**
1. *No. 03: Race Day Scheduling.* Lunch planned for: 1pm. Race starts: 2pm. Lunch served: 4:40pm. Number of times "two minutes" was said: 11.
2. *No. 08: Terminology.* Tick the phrases used at the table this week. [ ] "It's a good undercut." [ ] "He's got no pace." [ ] "Pass the potatoes, we're in the pit window." Score: 3 ticks, you are the pit crew.
3. *No. 12: Parc Fermé.* The sofa spot is not to be touched between lights out and the chequered flag. Persons permitted to touch it: 0. Persons who have: everyone, once.

**Certificate:** "Official Grand Prix Addict. Qualified to explain a strategy. Not qualified to carve."
