## The idea in one sentence

A mock self-help manual for a padel convert who has made the sport his whole personality, teaching him to talk about anything else at home and at work.

## Target buyer

Who actually pays for this? Not a demographic - a person.

Lauren: Josh's wife, who has two children under five, a job of her own and a husband who can't finish a sentence about the nursery fees without mentioning the bandeja. Or a colleague who has sat through one padel stat too many in the Monday stand-up and wants a Secret Santa that says it for the whole team. Or his brother, his best mate from uni, his mum. They like him a lot. They just want one conversation that doesn't end at the glass.

## Gift recipient

Who receives it, and what is the moment it gets handed over?

Josh: a UK professional in his late twenties or early thirties who took up padel about eighteen months ago and now plays four times a week, owns three rackets and runs the group chat. It gets handed over on his birthday, at Christmas, on his first Father's Day since he discovered padel, or at the office Secret Santa, where his whole team watches him open it and cheers.

## Recognition trigger

The "that's literally Dave" line. What specific, real behaviour will a reader
point at and say *that is exactly him*? List at least five.

1. Every conversation finds its way back to padel within four minutes, and he tells you, again, that it's "the fastest-growing sport in the world".
2. The padel WhatsApp group goes off at 6am with court bookings, and he answers it before he answers the baby.
3. Family weekends are planned around court slots. The kids' swimming lesson moved so he could keep his Saturday 9am.
4. He is trying to recruit everyone: his dad, his boss, the neighbours, the man who came to fix the boiler.
5. He wears padel kit to things that are not padel: a toddler's birthday party, a client lunch, his father-in-law's sixtieth.
6. The racket bag lives in the car boot next to the car seats and the nappy bag, "just in case a court comes up".
7. Every Monday stand-up opens with a "quick padel stat", and he has proposed padel as the team social three quarters running.
8. The family summer holiday is a "padel holiday" in Spain that he sold to Lauren as a family trip, because "the kids' club is right next to the courts".
9. He calls tennis "basically padel without the walls" in front of people who play tennis.
10. His smartwatch has more padel data on it than the NHS has on him, and he will show you the graphs.

## Humour angle

What is the comedic frame? (Parody manual? Rehabilitation programme? Field
guide? Medical textbook?) Why is that frame funnier than just "jokes about X"?

Josh is a management consultant, so the only language he still listens to is his own. The book is a consultancy engagement commissioned jointly by his two most exhausted stakeholders, Home (Lauren) and Work (his boss), to bring his padel talk back within tolerable limits. It comes as a deadpan programme pack: a scoping document, stakeholder interviews, a current-state assessment, a conversation budget, a change plan, a relapse risk register, a final review and a certificate of completion. Using the office frameworks he loves on the thing he can't stop talking about turns his own habits against him, and every chart and checklist becomes a fresh way to prove he has a problem.

The humour is medium: cheeky, pointed and written for adults, with the straight face doing most of the work. A little mild swearing is allowed for a punchline, as in the Golf book. The reader supplies the recognition.

## Commercial rationale

Why does this sell on Amazon? What does a buyer search for? What does it sit
next to on a shelf? What is the price point?

Padel is booming in the UK, with new clubs opening every month and a young, well-paid crowd who picked it up recently and can't stop talking about it. That means plenty of partners, colleagues and mates looking for a gift that takes the mickey. Buyers search for funny padel gifts, padel gifts for him, padel player gifts and padel Secret Santa. There are far fewer padel joke books than golf ones, so it has room to stand out. It sits beside hobby-obsession gift books, including our own Golf and Runner's Guides, and the title tells the joke at thumbnail size. Pricing in pounds is a later KDP decision.

## Constraints

Things this book must not do. Content limits, tone limits, anything that would
embarrass the buyer when it is opened at Christmas in front of their family.

UK throughout: British spelling, British words (mum, nursery, telly, tea for the evening meal) and prices in pounds. For adults, but mild enough to open at the office Secret Santa: swearing is rare and mild (bloody, sod, bollocks), used only for a punchline and never aimed at the family. Affectionate underneath: Josh is a good dad and a good husband who has a padel problem, never a bad one. No divorce or marriage-breakdown humour, and the children are never at risk or neglected, only unimpressed. No real padel brands, clubs, players, tournaments or apps by name, and no logos in the pictures. His employer is never a real firm. Generated art contains no text; all words are real type.
