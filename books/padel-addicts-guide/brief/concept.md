# Concept - The Padel Addict's Guide to Talking About Anything Else

> Locked at concept lock. Later stages may not renegotiate what is written here.

## Premise

Josh, 31, a management consultant with a wife, two children under five and a smartwatch full of padel data, took up padel eighteen months ago. Since then it has become his whole personality. His two main stakeholders, Home (his wife Lauren) and Work (his boss, Priya), have jointly commissioned an engagement to bring his padel talk back to a level the people around him can live with. This book is the programme pack. It runs from scoping to sign-off and ends with a Certificate of Completion, awarded on condition that he doesn't mention the certificate at padel.

## Structure of the book

How does the reader move through it? (Programme? Stages? Chapters as escalating
severity? A diagnosis-to-cure arc?)

An engagement in phases, each a chapter with an opener picture:

1. Scoping: why he's here, the stakeholder interviews (Lauren, Priya, his mum, the group chat), and a self-assessment of how far gone he is.
2. Current State: a day in the life of Josh, how quickly each conversation reaches padel, and what padel has replaced.
3. Talking About Anything Else: the core module. Topics other than padel, how to ask a question and listen to the answer, and a daily padel-mention budget.
4. The Home Front: bath time, weekends, the car boot, the group chat at 6am and what the children think Daddy's job is.
5. The Office: stand-ups, client lunches, the team social vote and LinkedIn.
6. Recruitment Ban: leaving his dad, the neighbours, the boiler man and the new starter alone.
7. Holidays and Occasions: the "family" trip to Spain, birthdays, Christmas and a wedding clash with a tournament.
8. Relapse Risk: triggers, warning signs and a plan for when the club opens a new court.
9. Sign-off: a final review with stakeholder feedback and the Certificate of Completion.

Each chapter mixes short text pages, checklists, diagnostic tests, case notes, comparisons and activity panels (ticks, scores, a gauge, a table), with the picture budget spent on chapter openers.

## Running jokes

Bits that reappear. Note where each one pays off.

- "The fastest-growing sport in the world": the programme keeps a tally of how often he says it. Pays off at Sign-off, where Mia says it first and he has to agree it's annoying.
- The four-minute rule: every conversation reaches padel in four minutes or less, measured across the chapters. Pays off at Sign-off when he gets through a whole dinner party and nobody mentions padel, until Lauren's dad asks him about it.
- The 6am group chat: short extracts from the padel WhatsApp group run through the book as evidence. Pays off with Josh leaving the chat on mute for one whole weekend.
- Stakeholder feedback: Lauren's and Priya's short, dry notes track his progress and relapses across the phases.
- The racket bag in the boot: it keeps being where something else should be (the buggy, the shopping, a birthday present). Pays off when he takes it out for the family holiday and the buggy goes in.
- Tennis is "padel without the walls": a slow-burning feud with Priya that ends with her beating him at tennis.

## What this book is NOT

A padel coaching book, a real guide to addiction, a book about a failing marriage, or jokes about bad shots on court. Padel is the setting; his family and his colleagues are the subject.
