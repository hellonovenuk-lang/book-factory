## Front matter

### Title page

The Padel Addict's Guide to Talking About Anything Else

A formal engagement to bring his padel talk back to a level that the people who live and work with him can survive.

### Notice of engagement

This engagement has been commissioned because two of your stakeholders have asked for it. They are Home and Work. It is the first project they have ever agreed on without a meeting.

The request followed a pattern. The pattern may involve a group chat at 6am, a racket bag in the car boot, a quick padel stat at the Monday stand-up, a quarter-zip at a christening, a "family" holiday to a resort with six courts, and the phrase "the fastest-growing sport in the world", said to a midwife.

Receiving this pack does not mean anybody is cross with you. Your first instinct to check whether it fits in the side pocket of your racket bag probably means somebody should be.

Keep the pack somewhere visible. Do not leave it in the car boot with the spare grips, in the changing room at the club, or on your desk at work, where Priya will find it and have it laminated.

### Terms of engagement

This engagement covers conversation only. It is not padel coaching and has no view on your vibora. Keep playing padel if padel makes you happy. The engagement simply asks that you also talk about other things, now and then, to the people who have to listen.

Any resemblance to a padel player you know is entirely intentional in spirit and entirely accidental in every way a solicitor would care about.

No padel club, brand, player or governing body has approved this engagement. Several would prefer you carried on exactly as you are.

### How a question works

A question is something one person asks another because they would like to know the answer. It has a subject. The subject is usually the thing mentioned in the question.

For example, if Lauren asks, "How was your day?" she would like to hear about your day. She does not need to hear about Thursday's court, which has not happened yet, or about Rob's new racket, which is not yours. If your mum asks, "How are the kids?" the answer is about the kids, and it does not end with "Mia's got great hand-eye, you know. Great padel hands."

The engagement suggests a simple method. Listen to the question. Find the subject. Answer about the subject. Stop. Then, if you would like to be really impressive, ask a question back.

### Words that belong on the court

### Listening

Listening is what happens when somebody else is talking and you are not waiting to talk about padel. Early participants found it strange. Several reported hearing things for the first time, such as their wife's colleagues' names, and the fact that Mia has a best friend called Ava, whose mum has invited you round twice.

It helps to know the signs. When you are listening, your eyes are on the person, your phone is face down and your hand is not quietly practising a backhand under the table.
