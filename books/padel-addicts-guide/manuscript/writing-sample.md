## Sample 1 - a typical internal passage

### Why you are here

This engagement was commissioned jointly by your two main stakeholders, Home and Work. It is the first thing they have ever agreed on without a meeting. Home is represented by Lauren, your wife. Work is represented by Priya, a senior partner at your firm, who plays tennis and would like that noted.

The brief is short. Over the last eighteen months you have mentioned padel in 94% of conversations lasting longer than four minutes. The other 6% were phone calls with the bank, and in two of those you asked the adviser whether she played. You have said "the fastest-growing sport in the world" 212 times, once to a midwife. Nobody is asking you to stop playing. Home and Work would simply like to know what else you think about, and they have paid for this engagement to find out.

The programme runs in nine phases, from Scoping to Sign-off. Each has exercises, a short assessment and feedback from your stakeholders. You will be tempted to treat the assessments like a padel ranking and try to top them. Please don't. The only good score in this book is a low one.

> **Stakeholder feedback (Home), week one.** Asked him how the nursery settling-in session went. He said Mia "read the room really well, like a good bandeja". Then he explained the bandeja. Alfie was eating a crayon at the time and Josh did not notice, because he was drawing the court on a napkin to show me the angles.

> **Stakeholder feedback (Work), week one.** Monday stand-up. Josh opened with a quick padel stat before the client update. The client update was about a client who was not happy with us. The padel stat was about a club in Leeds.

## Sample 2 - a structured bit (checklist, test, table copy)

### Current-state assessment

Award yourself one point for each statement that is true. Lauren will check your answers, and Priya has asked for a copy.

1. You have told someone that padel is the fastest-growing sport in the world, and they had not asked what the fastest-growing sport in the world was.
2. You answered the 6am group chat before you answered the baby.
3. The racket bag is in the car boot, and the buggy is not.
4. You moved Mia's swimming lesson so you could keep your Saturday 9am court.
5. You have worn a padel quarter-zip to a client lunch and described it as "smart casual".
6. You have tried to get your father-in-law into padel. He is seventy-one and has a new hip.
7. You have proposed padel as the team social three quarters running, and lost the vote to bowling each time.
8. You have shown someone your smartwatch graphs from Tuesday night's doubles, and they were not your doubles partner.
9. You have described tennis as "basically padel without the walls" to a person holding a tennis racket.
10. You are reading this list and working out how to explain that number 9 is technically true.

**Scoring**

- **0 to 2:** There may have been a mix-up at the printers. Please return this book to the person who gave it to you and apologise for their assumption.
- **3 to 6:** Moderate. The programme can help. Lauren describes herself as quietly hopeful, and asked us not to tell you.
- **7 to 9:** Severe. Mia has started calling your car "the padel car". Priya has started calling you "the padel one" in partner meetings.
- **10:** Bloody hell, Josh. Report to Phase One, and leave the racket in the boot.

## Sample 3 - a chapter opener

### Phase One: Scoping

Establishing the size of the problem, and who has been living with it.

Before any change can happen, the engagement needs to understand the current state. In this phase we will interview your stakeholders, map every place padel has turned up where it wasn't invited, and ask you to rate yourself honestly. The self-rating tends to come out lower than everyone else's, so we will ask Lauren too.

Please take the cap off for the stakeholder interviews. It is a padel cap, Josh. Everyone can see that it's a padel cap.

## Operator notes

What is right, what is wrong, what to change before the manuscript is written.

Drafted by Claude for Kieran's review (2026-09-26). The choices below are proposed for the manuscript:

- The programme speaks to the reader as "you", like the Golf and Runner's Guides, so any padel addict can be the reader. When the official voice cracks it calls him Josh, and Josh is the man in the pictures and in the stakeholder feedback.
- The consultancy frame shows in the structure (phases, stakeholders, a current-state assessment, sign-off), and the sentences themselves stay plain and dry, so the jokes read clearly.
- Stakeholder feedback comes from two voices, Home (Lauren) and Work (Priya), which lets every chapter land at home and in the office.
- Swearing is rare and mild, as agreed in the brief: one "bloody" saved for the last scoring line, and nothing stronger.
- UK throughout: nursery, Leeds, the telly, "tea" for the evening meal, prices in pounds.
