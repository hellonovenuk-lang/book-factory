## The idea in one sentence

The Golf Addict's Guide to Family Reintegration: a comic gift book that helps a golf-obsessed person relearn how to live with their family.

## Target buyer

Who actually pays for this? Not a demographic - a person.

A partner, grown-up son or daughter, or friend of a golfer who has quietly moved out to the course. They love the golfer, they have lost several weekends to him, and they want a gift that says so with a smile rather than a row.

## Gift recipient

Who receives it, and what is the moment it gets handed over?

An adult golf addict, most often a man aged 40 to 70, who plays several times a week and talks about it the rest of the time. It is handed over at a birthday, at Christmas or on Father's Day, ideally in front of the family who will read bits of it aloud.

## Recognition trigger

The "that's literally Dave" line. What specific, real behaviour will a reader
point at and say *that is exactly him*? List at least five.

1. "How was your day?" produces a hole-by-hole account of his round, including the putt on the 7th that lipped out.
2. He checks the weather forecast only to see whether Saturday's tee time is safe.
3. He "pops out for a quick nine" and comes back five hours later, surprised anyone noticed.
4. He practises his swing with whatever he is holding: an umbrella, a rolled-up newspaper, the barbecue tongs.
5. Family events are negotiated around tee times, and he knows the date of the club championship before his anniversary.
6. The family holiday is chosen because there happens to be a course nearby.
7. The garage, the car boot and the hallway all hold golf equipment, and none of it may be moved.
8. He watches golf on television on Sunday afternoon after playing golf on Sunday morning.

## Humour angle

What is the comedic frame? (Parody manual? Rehabilitation programme? Field
guide? Medical textbook?) Why is that frame funnier than just "jokes about X"?

The book is an official re-entry programme for someone returning to family life after a long posting abroad, where "abroad" is the golf club. It is written in the calm voice of an institution: induction notes, orientation sessions, household briefings, a relapse-prevention plan and a graduation certificate. Treating a man coming in from the 18th green as a returning expedition member makes every ordinary thing at home (a conversation, a birthday party, the lawnmower) a strange new culture he has to be taught. The straight face does the work; the reader supplies the recognition.

## Commercial rationale

Why does this sell on Amazon? What does a buyer search for? What does it sit
next to on a shelf? What is the price point?

Golf gifts sell steadily all year, with peaks at Christmas and Father's Day. Buyers search for golf gifts for men, funny golf gifts, golf gifts for dad and golf books humour. It sits beside novelty golf books and joke manuals, and it has more real content inside than most of them. The title explains the joke at thumbnail size. Pricing is a later KDP decision.

## Constraints

Things this book must not do. Content limits, tone limits, anything that would
embarrass the buyer when it is opened at Christmas in front of their family.

Affectionate throughout: the golfer is loved and missed, never mocked as a bad father or husband. No drinking jokes beyond a mild clubhouse mention, no marriage-breakdown or divorce humour, nothing crude. No real golf brands, clubs, courses or professional players by name, and no logos in the pictures. Generated art contains no text; all words are real type. ## Visual concept (first thoughts)

Rough direction only - the visual bible is where this gets decided properly.

Classic editorial caricature in a fine ink line with muted colour washes: greens, sand, cream and soft navy. The recurring golfer is drawn the same in every picture: a recognisable face, a golf cap he never takes off and a single glove in his back pocket. Chapter openers show him in family settings he does not quite fit, often still in golf gear. The cover shows him at the family dinner table, mid-swing with a bread stick.
