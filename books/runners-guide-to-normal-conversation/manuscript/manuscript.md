Reason for referral
It now occupies breakfast, weekends, online activity, family
logistics and the first twelve minutes of any conversation
with a person careless enough to ask how you are. Friends
know which toenail is causing concern. Colleagues know
your target race. Your partner can distinguish a recovery
run from an easy run but wishes they could not.

 No single behaviour caused the referral. The Service acts
when the behaviours form a training block.

  Common referral sources include a colleague trapped
beside the kettle, a partner who has moved a holiday twice,
and a family group chat containing more route screenshots
than photographs of family members.

The ordinary question test




                Pat only asked about the weekend.
A normal person hears: “Did you have a nice weekend?”
They say: “Yes, thanks. Quiet one. How about you?”

  You hear the same question and begin at 06:15 on
Sunday. You explain the porridge, the weather app, the
missing sock, the first three kilometres, the decision not to
chase pace, the pace you accidentally chased, the headwind
near the retail park and the reason the final average was
not representative.

  The question has not been answered. It has been outlived.

Case file: Alex
Alex is a recreational runner of ordinary talent and
exceptional administrative commitment.

  On referral, Alex owned two formal outfits and eleven
garments described as layers. Their watch had recorded a

to prove this, all of which were about running.

  Dr Hughes recorded the patient as cooperative, articulate

### PDF page 14

Baseline conversation recording
Have a five-minute conversation with a willing adult. Do not
tell them this is an assessment; runners perform unnaturally
when they know a result will be recorded.

Afterwards, mark every topic that appeared.

     The other person’s day

     News

     Family

     Work

     Food

     A shared plan

     Running

     The difference between chip time and gun time

     Why your current pace is misleading

     The camber on one particular road




If the final four topics occupied more than half the conversation, the
baseline has been established. If the other person left before five minutes,
record the duration honestly. Do not pause the clock while they make tea.

The wrist device
The fitness watch began as a tool. It now sits at the centre
of a small constitutional arrangement.

  You consult it when waking, walking, eating and waiting
for a lift. It informs you that you slept badly, which surprises
you despite having been awake for most of it checking
whether you were asleep. It assigns a recovery score to a
body you have inhabited for decades.

  At formal events, the watch remains. Suits, dresses and
carefully chosen jewellery must negotiate around a black
rubber strap and a face displaying an urgent green number.

Patients often report a sensation that somebody may have

Technical clothing migration




Running kit reproduces in cupboards.

   First comes a pair of shorts. Then winter tights, summer

video calls and compression socks on a flight long enough to
justify mentioning them.

### PDF page 25

Weather interpretation




Non-runners experience weather as a condition.

  You experience it as a session variable.

  Rain is cooling. Wind is resistance. Frost is ‘fine once you
get moving’. Heat is an opportunity to practise hydration. A
yellow warning means the route will be quieter.

  The Service recognises your resilience. The people
watching you run through horizontal sleet in five-inch shorts
recognise something else.

  When somebody says, ‘Awful weather,’ reply, ‘It really is.’

   Do not add ‘although’. Nothing helpful has ever followed
‘although’ in this context.

### PDF page 30

T H E R U N N E R ’ S G U I D E T O N O R M A L C O N V E R S AT I O N




Receive a compliment without
supplying data
At some point, a kind person will say that you are looking
well. They are offering a compliment. They have not
requested your resting heart rate.

     Begin with the words ‘Thank you.’ Stop there.

   You may notice a powerful urge to explain that your
watch disagrees because your training readiness is only 46.
This sensation is common and will pass. Do not raise your
wrist. Do not describe last night’s sleep score. The person
complimenting you has already taken a considerable social
risk and should not be made to interpret a coloured
semicircle.

  One patient successfully accepted a compliment at a
wedding, then ruined the result by adding that the suit felt
loose because marathon training had ‘melted the last bit
off’. Progress was recorded as administrative only.

### PDF page 32

T H E R U N N E R ’ S G U I D E T O N O R M A L C O N V E R S AT I O N




The return question
Conversational recovery depends on returning attention to
the other person.

   After one statement about yourself, ask a relevant
question about them. Listen to the answer without scanning
it for entry points.

     Sam: We went to York at the weekend. You: Was it good?

     The following responses fail the exercise:

     ‘I ran York once.’

     ‘York is flatter than people think.’

     ‘Did you go near the racecourse?’

     ‘I’ve always said it would be a good marathon city.’

     ‘How many steps did you do?’

  The question belongs to Sam. Allow York to remain
unmeasured.

Listening posture




Lower the wrist.

  Turn your body towards the speaker.

  Keep the phone out of your hand.

  Allow your face to respond to their words rather than to a
vibration from an app.

  These actions may feel theatrical. For non-runners they
form part of listening.

  Sam reports that Alex’s early listening posture resembled
waiting for a pedestrian crossing. Improvement followed
once Alex stopped bouncing gently on the toes ‘to stay
loose’.

### PDF page 36

Emergency stop phrases
When you detect a run report already in progress, use one
of the following.

     ‘Sorry, I’ve gone into the splits.’

     ‘You only asked whether I was free on Sunday.’

     ‘That is more detail than the story needed.’

     ‘I’ll spare you the shoe explanation.’

     ‘Please continue with your own news.’




Say the phrase once. Do not turn the self-correction into a funny story
about how much you talk about running. That story is also about running.

### PDF page 37

B A S I C C O N V E R S AT I O N R E H A B I L I TAT I O N




A controlled disclosure
You may share running news when it matters to you.
Recovery does not require secrecy; it requires proportion.

  Use this structure:

  1. State the news.

  2. State why you care.

  3. Stop.

  4. Let the listener choose whether to ask more.

  Example:

   ‘I got a place in the London Marathon. I’ve wanted to do
it for years, so I’m delighted.’

  This is complete. Training volume, charity targets, hotel
availability and the ballot system remain available if
requested.

  An expression of warmth is a response, not informed
consent for the route plan.

Re-entry
You are now ready to re-enter places where nobody has
been assigned a pace.

   This stage covers restaurants, offices, weddings, family
holidays and other environments that continue to operate
despite having no start gantry. You will practise arriving
without having run there, choosing food for its taste and
allowing somebody else’s weekend to remain about them.

  Early exposure can be disorientating. Restaurants may
provide no secure area for a hydration vest. Hotel
receptionists often do not know the elevation profile of the
bypass. At weddings, the people dressed alike near the front
are usually members of the wedding party, not pacers.

  Before attending any social event, remove one piece of
technical clothing. Advanced patients may attempt trousers
with no zipped rear pocket.

### PDF page 41

The office kitchen




The office kitchen is a high-risk environment because
innocent prompts occur near witnesses.

  Pat: You’re in early.

   A healthy response might concern traffic, workload or
sleep.

  Your current response begins, ‘Had to get ten in before
the first call.’

  Enter the kitchen after a morning run. Make tea. If asked
why your hair is wet, answer truthfully in six words or
fewer.

  Suggested answer: ‘I ran in. It was raining.’

  Unsuitable answer: ‘I ran in, but kept it properly easy
because…’

  Six words have passed. Drink the tea.

Meetings
Your colleagues have accepted the fitness watch. They have
not accepted it as a second chair.

During a meeting:

     Disable lap alerts.

     Do not interpret stress notifications aloud.

     Do not stand because the watch instructed you to
     move.

     Do not announce that your body battery fell during
     finance.

     Do not refer to the meeting as mental endurance
     work.




If a calendar invitation clashes with training, move the training privately.
The department does not need to know that threshold has been displaced.
Pat once received an email titled ‘Tuesday reshuffle’ and assumed a
project deadline had changed. It concerned intervals.

Restaurants
A menu is not a fuelling plan with prices.

  Choose something because you would like to eat it. Avoid
interviewing the waiter about the dry weight of the rice. A
meal can contain carbohydrates without becoming carb
loading; the distinction is important to everybody except
your digestive system.

  Complete one meal without saying:

  ‘I’ve earned this.’

  ‘I need the salt.’

  ‘Good recovery food.’

  ‘This might sit a bit heavy tomorrow.’

  ‘I’m not normally this bad.’

  You are eating chips, Alex. No governing body has opened
an inquiry.

Weddings and formal occasions




Formalwear creates three common difficulties.

  First, the fitness watch does not match. You will wear it
anyway and explain that the data streak cannot be broken
for a wedding.

  Second, a relative may ask about ‘all the running’. Treat
this as a courtesy question, not a keynote invitation.

   Third, the dance floor will affect your step count. Do not
circle the room at 23:52 to reach a round number. People
remember that.

  Allow the watch battery to fall below 20 per cent without
asking the venue for a charger.

   If the bride offers one, the failure has already become
visible.

### PDF page 45

Holidays




A holiday is a temporary visit to another place. It need not
become an overseas training camp.

  Before booking accommodation, each member of the
party may name two essential features. ‘Near a flat, well-lit
10 km loop’ counts as one of yours, not one of everyone’s.

  On arrival, refrain from saying the roads look runnable.
Roads generally are.

Family events

Other people’s weekends
Ask somebody what they did at the weekend.

The ‘just an easy one’
intervention




The phrase appears modest. It contains distance, pace and a
pre-emptive defence against an accusation nobody made.

  ‘Yes, it was nice.’

  Silence.

  If they do not ask, place it carefully back inside yourself.
