## Chapters

### Chapter 1 — Referral and Diagnosis
**Job:** Establish the referral frame and the basic pattern of conversational capture.
**Recognition:** A weekend question becomes an account of porridge, pace and headwind.
**PDF pages:** 7–16.

### Chapter 2 — Identifying Your Triggers
**Job:** Identify the watch, routes, clothes and routine that turn small talk back to running.
**Recognition:** The watch gets a vote in a social interaction.
**PDF pages:** 17–26.

### Chapter 3 — Basic Conversation Rehabilitation
**Job:** Practise short answers, listening and asking a question back.
**Recognition:** An ordinary account of a run needs a strict time limit.
**PDF pages:** 27–38.

### Chapter 4 — Life Outside the Training Plan
**Job:** Apply the practice in work, meals, travel and home life.
**Recognition:** A holiday is quietly assessed for routes and the watch accompanies formal occasions.
**PDF pages:** 39–50.

### Chapter 5 — Equipment, Economics and Other Symptoms
**Job:** Examine shoe buying, kit justifications and the economics of a hobby that fills the hallway.
**Recognition:** Each of many shoes has a supposedly indispensable role.
**PDF pages:** 51–60.

### Chapter 6 — Advanced Social Exposure
**Job:** Let other people run, speak and make plans without turning their news into a report on one’s own training.
**Recognition:** A friend’s achievement risks becoming a comparison.
**PDF pages:** 61–70.

### Chapter 7 — Relapse Prevention and Conditional Discharge
**Job:** Spot the small phrases that restart the monologue and make a realistic, conditional discharge.
**Recognition:** “Just an easy one” carries an entire pace defence.
**PDF pages:** 71–76.
