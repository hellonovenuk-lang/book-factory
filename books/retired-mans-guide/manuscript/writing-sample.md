## Sample 1 - a typical internal passage

### Welcome to the Household

Congratulations on your new position. After forty-one years in operations you have joined the Household, a small but well-established organisation that has run without interruption since 1985. You may have assumed you were coming in to manage it. Please read this handbook before acting on that assumption.

The Household is led by Sue, Head of Household Operations. Sue has no job title, no annual review and no deputy, and in thirty-nine years she has never once been late with a birthday card. She runs the kitchen, the diary, the garden, the family WhatsApp group, the Christmas list for nine people and Biscuit, who is eleven. You will report to her. Biscuit, for now, will not report to you.

Your first week will feel strange. You will wake at 6:15 because your body still thinks there is a train to catch. You will come downstairs, stand in the kitchen in your gilet, and ask what we are doing today. This is normal, and everyone who has joined the Household from full-time work has asked it. The answer, on most weekdays, is nothing in particular, and Sue would like to keep it that way.

> **Note from Household Operations, day one.** He was dressed by quarter to seven. He asked whether I had "a plan for the day". I said I was going to have a cup of tea. He asked what time. Biscuit got up, looked at him for a while, and went back to bed.

> **Note from Household Operations, day three.** The cupboards have been "rationalised". The tins are now in date order. The colander is gone.

## Sample 2 - a structured bit (checklist, test, table copy)

### Day 1 assessment: are you ready to be home?

Tick each statement that is true. Sue will mark it, and her marking is final.

1. You have asked "what are we doing today?" before eight o'clock in the morning.
2. You have reloaded the dishwasher after somebody else loaded it, and said you weren't criticising.
3. You have moved something in the kitchen "so it makes more sense", and Sue has since spent four minutes looking for it.
4. You have started a project that was meant to take a weekend. It was the weekend of 14 March.
5. You have been to the tip more than twice in one week and recognised one of the staff.
6. You have followed Sue round the supermarket and pointed out that the big tin of beans is better value per hundred grams.
7. You have watched the lunchtime weather forecast and then reported it to the household, unasked.
8. You have told an old colleague on the phone that you are "busier than ever", and then described the bins.
9. You have arrived at nursery to collect your granddaughter eleven minutes early and waited in the car with the engine running.
10. You have read this list and wanted to point out that number 5 is efficient use of a council facility.

**Scoring**

- **0 to 2:** You may still be at work. Please check, and if so, go back.
- **3 to 6:** A normal start. The Household has seen worse, and Sue is cautiously optimistic, although she has asked us not to tell you.
- **7 to 9:** An eager start. Sue has hidden the tape measure. Biscuit has started sleeping upstairs.
- **10:** Bloody hell, Graham. Put the kettle on, sit down, and go to Stage One.

## Sample 3 - a chapter opener

### Stage Three: The Kitchen

The most important room in the Household, and the one with the most rules you didn't know about.

For forty years the kitchen has run very well without you. You have used it at breakfast, at tea and to find biscuits. It now has a second person in it from seven in the morning, and that person has a tape measure on his belt. This stage covers who decides where things go, what "helping" means, and the Dishwasher Loading Review, which you are encouraged to approach with an open mind.

Sue has put the colander back where it was. Please leave it there.

## Operator notes

What is right, what is wrong, what to change before the manuscript is written.

Drafted by Claude for Kieran's review (2026-10-02). The choices below are proposed for the manuscript:

- The handbook speaks to the reader as "you", like the Golf and Padel Guides, so any newly retired man can be the reader. When the official voice cracks it calls him Graham, and Graham is the man in the pictures and the notes.
- The frame is an induction pack for a new starter: stages, a Day 1 assessment, probation and a Treaty at the end. The sentences stay plain and dry.
- The second voice is "Note from Household Operations", Sue's short dated notes. Biscuit the dog appears in them as a silent witness.
- Nothing about age, health, money or death, as fixed at intake: Graham is fit, capable and busy, just busy with the wrong things.
- Swearing is rare and mild: one "bloody" for the last scoring line.
- UK throughout: the tip, the big shop, nursery, tea, prices in pounds.
